Principles

---------------------------------------------------

Firmitas, Utilitas, Venustus
Durability, Function and Beauty
(Vetruvius, circa 50 BCE)

Translation of Vitruvius often mis-translates these 3 basics. They are an expression of professionalism in the field of design, even before it existed as a proper profession as such. The key is not allowing any 1 of these 3 components to over-ride and allow another to be forgotten. Beauty with utility, longevity with function, and durability with aesthetics.

In construction it is often noted that the difficulty in completing building projects calls for a selection of 'Quality, Time, Price: pick two'. Vitruvius' dictum tells us, except perhaps in exceptional circumstances when the program specifically says otherwise, that in order to be a proper professional one must deliver on all three cournerstones of architectural design: Firmitas, Utilitas & Venustus.

---------------------------------------------------

Plan Conservatively & Execute Ambitiously

- in architecture, if the original design is too close to the limits of construction capability than you will have no wiggle room during project execution, either to deal with changing project conditions or changes/improvements you want mid-project. One plans conservatively to allow ambition in execution.

(Frank Lloyd Wright was terrible at this, which is why, for example, the Villa Savoy & Falling Water were barey inhabited by actual purchasing residential users. The former leaked badly and the latter had structural and moisture problems from day one. The Robie house in Chicago had dining room chairs so uncomfortable as to be barely usable and extensive glass doors in the panormic living room that could not keep out the harsh winds of a Chicago winter. FLW was, however, a brilliantly ambitious planner)

Primary and Secondary Objectives

(1) Divide projects into Primary and Secondary Objectives
(2) Fulfill Primary objectives first, then get in what secondary objectives you can

(if you can't do (1) because multiple considerations are more or less equal then (2) will not apply. However there is pretty much always Primary vs Secondary/Tertiary objectives)

This I learned designing classrooms and lecture halls for UofT.
The trend starting in the late 2000's toward more interactive space (expanding on conventional lecture halls and seminar rooms) had been tried before, and the UofT inventiory of buildings and rooms had all the variations (well - lots of layout variations before our modern advances in a/v).
Analysis of my own attempts and of past sucesses and failures showed that attempts to make one space do everything equally were typically failures. Therefore one designs, still, a lecture hall primarily for presentation and then secondarily for interacivity (otherwise tyour square footage quickly goes out the window) and a seminar room primarily for interaction and secondarily for presentation.
(For efficiency the University will keep trying to increase the seminar room capacity until the personal interactivity is compromised, and will likelwise try to add interactivity to the lecture hall to the point of disregarding the performance of the primary presentation component)
